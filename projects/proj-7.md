Contemporary Japanese Timber Construction

Location: Belgrade, Serbia · Year: 2018 · Type: University Project

The project explores the design of a contemporary gallery through timber construction, structural repetition, and principles derived from traditional and contemporary Japanese architecture. The aim was to investigate how a relatively simple wooden structural system could create spatial richness through layering, rhythm, light, and the relationship between interior and exterior.

The project began with a question: How can traditional timber principles be translated into a contemporary architectural language without simply reproducing historic forms?

Research into Japanese wooden architecture formed the basis of the design, with particular attention given to the relationship between structure and architectural expression. Contemporary interpretations of Japanese timber construction — especially the work of Kengo Kuma and his exploration of layered wooden roof systems — became an important reference during the development of the project.

The building is organized around a regular timber structural system, while the roof becomes its most expressive element. Repeated wooden members overlap and extend beyond the enclosed volume, creating a deep canopy that filters sunlight and gradually dissolves the boundary between architecture and the surrounding landscape.

At the centre of the pavilion, the timber construction becomes more sculptural. A vertically layered wooden element connects the different levels and transforms the structural language of the roof into an internal spatial experience. Rather than concealing construction, the project uses the repetition and connection of individual wooden elements as part of the architectural character.

The gallery spaces remain deliberately open and flexible, allowing artwork to be displayed against more restrained interior surfaces while the exposed timber structure provides warmth, rhythm, and texture. Large glazed openings strengthen the connection with the surrounding park and allow daylight and vegetation to become part of the gallery atmosphere.

Above, a sheltered roof terrace extends the gallery into the landscape. The oversized timber canopy creates a protected outdoor room where visitors can move between enclosed exhibition space and an open, contemplative environment.

Developed under the mentorship of Prof. Ljiljana Đukanović and Asst. Dijana Savanović during the summer semester of 2018, the project became an exploration of how construction itself can become architectural expression — using timber not simply as a material, but as a system for creating space, atmosphere, and identity.
