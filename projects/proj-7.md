Contemporary Japanese Timber Construction

Location: Belgrade, Serbia · Year: 2018 · Type: University Project

The project explores a contemporary gallery through timber construction, structural repetition, and principles inspired by traditional and contemporary Japanese architecture. The central question was: How can traditional timber principles be translated into a contemporary architectural language without simply reproducing historic forms?

A regular timber structure forms the basis of the building, while the roof becomes its most expressive element. Layered wooden members extend beyond the enclosed volume to create a deep canopy that filters light, frames views, and strengthens the relationship between interior and landscape. The influence of contemporary Japanese timber architecture, particularly the work of Kengo Kuma, informed the structural and spatial language.

Open gallery spaces, large glazed surfaces, and a sheltered roof terrace create a continuous transition between exhibition space and the surrounding park. Developed under the mentorship of Prof. Ljiljana Đukanović and Asst. Dijana Savanović in 2018, the project explores timber not only as a material, but as a system for creating space, atmosphere, and architectural identity.
