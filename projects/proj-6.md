Luxury Housing & Cantilevered Living Spaces

Location: Belgrade, Serbia · Year: 2018 · Type: University Project

Located in the centre of Vračar, close to the Temple of Saint Sava, the project explores contemporary urban housing within one of Belgrade’s most desirable residential areas. The proposal responds to the dense city context by creating generous apartments with strong visual and spatial connections to the surrounding neighbourhood, nearby greenery, and one of Belgrade’s most recognizable landmarks.

The project began with a question: How can outdoor space become an integral part of urban living rather than simply an addition to the apartment?

The architectural concept is based on a series of shifted volumes that extend beyond the main building envelope, forming large elevated terraces and outdoor rooms. Their position changes from floor to floor, creating different relationships with the surroundings while opening selected views toward the Temple of Saint Sava and the surrounding green areas.

An expressive steel structural system supports these cantilevered volumes and becomes an important part of the architectural identity. Rather than concealing the structure, diagonal steel members are integrated into the façade and terraces, establishing a rhythm that reflects the shifting geometry of the building.

The transparent building envelope is complemented by movable perforated metal panels that provide adaptable solar protection and privacy. Their abstract pattern draws inspiration from the architectural ornamentation associated with Saint Sava, establishing a subtle connection between the contemporary residential building and its local context. As the panels move across the glazed façade, the appearance of the building changes throughout the day.

Inside, the apartments are organized around open living spaces with extensive glazing and direct access to the terraces. The aim was to blur the boundary between interior and exterior, allowing the elevated outdoor spaces to function as extensions of everyday domestic life rather than conventional balconies.

The project was developed under the mentorship of Prof. Shin Yokoo, Prof. Vladimir Lojanica and Asst. Ksenija Pantović during the winter semester of 2018.
